\# SkillBridge — Project Plan

\## 1. Project Overview

\*\*Project name:\*\* SkillBridge

\*\*Project type:\*\* Student talent discovery directory

\*\*Purpose:\*\* SkillBridge helps students discover other students by their skills and interests, explore their profiles, and identify people they may want to connect with.

\## 2. The Problem

Students can have useful skills in areas such as web development, graphic design, writing, photography, and video editing without an easy way for other students to discover those abilities.

SkillBridge will provide a simple directory where students can explore skills and learn about the people who have them.

\## 3. Target Users

University students who want to discover other students with useful skills, explore talent within their student community, or find people with shared interests.

\## 4. The Core User Flow

1\. A visitor opens the student directory.

2\. The visitor searches for a student or skill.

3\. The visitor filters the displayed profiles by skill.

4\. The visitor opens a profile to learn more about the student.

5\. The visitor can save a profile as a favourite.

\## 5. Week 1 Scope

\- Establish the React development environment.

\- Create the initial project plan and identify the main risks.

\- Set up a public GitHub repository and starter README.

\- Sketch the directory screen.

\- Build a responsive first screen.

\- Add two JavaScript interactions.

\- Handle empty or invalid search input.

\- Document how to run the application and its expected behaviour.

\## 6. Initial Data Approach

The first version will use fictional student profiles authored by the developer. It will not collect real students' personal information or require a backend.

\## 7. Out of Scope for the First Version

\- User registration and login.

\- Student profile submission.

\- Payments or subscriptions.

\- Real-time messaging.

\- A production database.

\- Public launch as a real student service.

These features may be considered after the BuildLab project and only when appropriate.

## 9. Top Three Project Risks

\### Risk 1: Misuse of the Platform

\- \*\*Risk:\*\* Some users could misuse SkillBridge to harass other students, send unwanted romantic messages, impersonate people, or scam them.

\- \*\*Impact:\*\* This could make students feel unsafe, damage trust in the platform, harm its reputation, and potentially lead to complaints or the university restricting its use.

\- \*\*Mitigation:\*\* During BuildLab, I will use fictional student profiles and avoid publishing real students' personal contact information. Before launching a real platform, I will consider reporting and moderation features, clear community guidelines, and a process for removing abusive profiles.

\### Risk 2: Student Privacy

\- \*\*Risk:\*\* Students may not have enough control over the information displayed on their profiles or who can find them through directory searches.

\- \*\*Impact:\*\* Students may feel that their personal information is exposed without their permission, reducing their trust in SkillBridge.

\- \*\*Mitigation:\*\* The BuildLab version will use fictional profiles and will not collect real student information. Before launching a real platform, I will consider profile visibility settings, user consent, and controls that allow students to limit who can view or find their profiles.

\### Risk 3: Website Performance

\- \*\*Risk:\*\* If visitor numbers grow beyond what the application and hosting environment can handle, the website may become slow or unavailable.

\- \*\*Impact:\*\* Visitors may experience slow loading, errors, or difficulty accessing student profiles, resulting in a poor user experience.

\- \*\*Mitigation:\*\* I will keep the first version lightweight, use optimized assets, and test the application before sharing it widely. If usage grows significantly, I will monitor performance and consider improving the hosting infrastructure or application architecture.

A visitor can open the directory, search and filter fictional student profiles, and understand the purpose of the application. The initial screen works on mobile and desktop, and its interactions provide useful feedback when the search produces no results or receives empty input.
